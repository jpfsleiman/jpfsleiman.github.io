I'm a 2026–2027 AGI/AAAS Congressional Science & Engineering Fellow in the office of Representative Paul D. Tonko (NY-20), one of about 30 Congressional Fellows nationwide in the AAAS Science & Technology Policy Fellowships.

In the office I help write legislation, draft memos, speeches, and oversight letters, prepare hearing questions, and work with agencies, constituents, and stakeholders. My portfolio covers energy, water and natural resources, national parks and cultural preservation, museums and libraries, critical minerals and battery recycling, and Tribal issues. I also support the Member's committee and caucus work, including Energy and Commerce and the Museum Caucus.

Before the Hill, I was an AGU Local Science Partner from 2025 to 2026, working with congressional offices in South Carolina and New York: preparing appropriations requests for NASA, NOAA, NSF, USGS, and DOE, securing Dear Colleague signatures, and organizing district meetings. I also wrote 15 science policy op-eds for the Campus Times and published a policy memo on NASA's Regional Planetary Image Facility network in the Journal of Science Policy & Governance.

I hold a Ph.D. in Geophysics from the University of Rochester (2026), where I used satellite radar, LiDAR, and machine learning to study slow-moving slope hazards in permafrost regions.

Running through all of it

  • Speech and debate coach, since 2017. I founded and coach competitive programs in under-resourced high schools in South Carolina and New York, training students in Congressional and World Schools Debate. Several teams have qualified for national tournaments.

Outside of work

I was born in Greenville, South Carolina. Away from the office I collect rocks (as any good geologist should), read, work on cars, do amateur carpentry, and photograph the night sky.
