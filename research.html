Projects

Hillslope failure and soil displacement

Ph.D. research, University of Rochester

What happens to a hillslope when the frozen ground underneath it warms? I built laboratory experiments that reproduce Arctic temperature cycles in indoor climate chambers, then measured frost heave and ice lens formation in controlled soil at sub-millimeter precision. The work helps explain how temperature influences the stability of slopes in permafrost regions.

Lobate features on Mars

Ph.D. research, University of Rochester

Solifluction lobes are large soil patterns that form on cold hillslopes on Earth through frost heave. Similar patterns appear on Mars and may record its past environment, but it was unclear whether they form the same way. I profiled more than 3,000 terrain features across 26 sites on both planets and found that the Martian features follow the same scaling as the ones on Earth.

Tracking ground movement in Norway from orbit

Ph.D. research, with Norway's national ground-motion monitoring program

I analyzed 10 years of satellite radar data to track how fast slopes creep across permafrost regions in Norway, and tested how temperature, precipitation, and slope conditions control where hazards emerge.

Can student-collected soil data show long-term trends?

NASA GLOBE Program internship, 2025

GLOBE collects environmental measurements from students and educators around the world. I processed more than 4 million soil moisture and temperature measurements to test whether the record has the coverage needed to detect long-term trends, identified the schools with enough sustained data to be usable, and advised educators on keeping data quality up over time.

Predicting storm-driven power outages

StormImpact internship, 2022

I helped build a cloud-based machine learning tool that predicts storm-driven power outages for utility companies and guides emergency crew deployment.

Radio emission from supernova remnants

Undergraduate research with Dr. David Moffett, Furman University

I analyzed radio observations from the Very Large Array alongside archival X-ray data to study the remnants of exploded stars, and wrote a funded proposal for more telescope time.

Magnesian calcite as a record of past environments

Senior thesis with Dr. Christopher Romanek, Furman University, funded by the SC NASA Space Grant Consortium

I ran 48 controlled experiments growing calcium carbonate under different water chemistries to study how minerals record the conditions they formed in, with an eye toward reading the chemistry of ancient oceans on Mars.
